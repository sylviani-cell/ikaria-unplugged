CHAPTER 01 / OCTOBER 17–18

A Different Relationship With Work

Being busy and feeling rushed are not necessarily the same thing.

Before arriving in Ikaria, I knew that the island was famous for its residents’ longevity. I had heard about the Mediterranean diet, physical activity, and the importance of community. However, I was curious to see what these aspects of the Ikarian lifestyle actually looked like in everyday life.

One of the first things I noticed was how different the island felt from the places I was used to. The roads were narrow and winding, the landscape was mountainous, and everything seemed much more spread out. It wasn’t exactly the kind of place where you could easily rush from one activity to another.

During our first few days, we visited a pottery workshop by the sea, where we learned to make cups and bowls using the pinch method. We also visited a honey hut, where we learned about beekeeping and honey production. These visits gave us a chance to see a small part of what everyday work looked like for some of the people living on the island.

Something that stood out to me was how relaxed the people we met seemed, even while managing their businesses and teaching our group. We learned that many people on the island held multiple jobs, so it wasn’t necessarily that they had fewer responsibilities. Rather, I noticed that their interactions with us felt less hurried than what I was accustomed to in the United States.

Of course, we were visitors, and these interactions only offered a limited glimpse into their lives. I couldn’t know how much stress they experienced outside of the time we spent together. Still, the contrast made me think about how easily I associate being busy with feeling rushed.

As the trip continued, I became more interested in how people balanced their daily responsibilities with spending time with others and enjoying what they were doing. It was something I would continue to notice throughout our time in Ikaria.
